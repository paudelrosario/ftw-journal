# Journal — 10-04-2025 — Data Quality & Review of DE Workflow

## 1) What I learned
- Data Quality (DQ) is not a one-time thing; it should be implemented across the process, with double checks.  
- Checks → Monitoring → Modeling  
- Use layered checks, log everything, and show data health.  
- Quality → Trust  
- AI amplifies bad inputs → bigger, faster mistakes  
- Referential integrity: every `id_assessment` should exist in `assessments`.  

**Where DQ lives in the stack:**  
1. **Ingestion (raw)** — schema/type, volume, freshness (number of rows/columns)  
2. **Transform (clean)** — nulls, categories, foreign keys  
3. **Mart** — reasonableness, uniqueness, business rules  

**The DQ Contract:**  
- Define requirements, thresholds, and turnaround time.  

**What to put on Dashboards:**  
- "Updated as of" tiles  
- Data health card: % tests passed, freshness, nulls  

**DQ Health Dashboard Metrics:**  
- % tests passed (by dataset, by rule type)  
- Freshness lag (minutes/hours)  
- Volume vs baseline (z-score)  
- Null % on critical fields  

**Other**
Dimensional modeling → easier queries, avoid too many joins (better 1–2 joins minimum).  

## 2) New vocabulary (define in your own words)
- **Quality: Accuracy** — correct, error-free, dependable  
- **Quality: Completeness** — comprehensive, no missing/obsolete records  
- **Quality: Reliability** — extracted from reliable sources; consistent, without contradictions  
- **Quality: Relevancy** — relevant for the purpose it was collected  
- **Quality: Timeliness** — up-to-date and readily available  

- **Incident workflow** — MTTD (Mean Time to Detect) / MTTR (Mean Time to Resolve)  
- **Orchestration** — automation
